COMFORT BEFORE UPGRADES

Find your driving position.

A comfortable, repeatable position helps you steer and use the pedals smoothly. You do not need a racing seat or a fixed set of measurements: use these checks as a starting point, then adjust for your body, equipment, and any comfort needs.

Set the seat, wheel and pedals.

Set your sitting position first, then move the controls to meet you. Make one adjustment at a time and test it with a short, familiar session.

01 / BACK AND SEAT

Sit supported, not stiff

Keep your hips settled in the seat and your back supported. A slight recline is fine if you can still see the screen clearly and reach the wheel without lifting your shoulders. Avoid a position that makes you slide forward or round your back.

02 / WHEEL REACH

Leave a little bend in your arms

With your shoulders resting against the seat, reach toward the wheel at roughly the 9-and-3 hand positions. Adjust the seat or wheel so your elbows remain softly bent and your shoulders stay relaxed through turns. Do not lock your elbows or stretch your upper body forward.

03 / PEDAL DISTANCE

Keep your knees comfortably bent

Place your feet on the pedals and press each one through its full travel. At full brake, your knee should still have a bend and your hips should stay against the seat. Move the pedals closer if your leg straightens, or farther away if your knee is cramped.

04 / PEDAL ANGLE

Use a natural ankle motion

Set the pedals so you can press and release smoothly without pointing your toes hard, twisting your ankle, or lifting your heel uncomfortably. A slightly raised pedal face is a reasonable starting point, but comfort and full control matter more than an exact angle.

05 / SCREEN AND CHECK

Make the view easy to hold

Place the screen where you can read the track and instruments while keeping your head and neck relaxed. Do a few laps, checking that you can steer, brake, and use the throttle without shifting in your seat. Reposition anything that slides.

06 / ADJUST TO YOU

Comfort wins over measurements

Body proportions, mobility, and equipment differ. Treat any angle or distance in a setup photo as an example, not a rule. Stop and change the position if you feel pain, tingling, numbness, or persistent strain.

Using a regular office chair?

You can get a steady starting position without buying a cockpit. Prioritize a chair and controls that stay put when you brake.

01 / ANCHOR THE CHAIR

Keep it from rolling

Use a stable floor surface and, if the chair has casters, a suitable caster cup or chair mat designed to stop movement. Do not rely on loose objects wedged under the wheels. Check the chair stays still during a firm brake press.

02 / SECURE PEDALS

Stop the pedal set sliding

Put pedals on a non-slip surface and against a solid stop if the manufacturer permits it. Avoid balancing the pedals on stacks or unstable furniture. Recheck that the pedal platform cannot tip or move.

03 / SET THE REACH

Move the chair before stretching

Bring the chair close enough that your elbows and knees remain bent, then check your monitor and wheel position. Use a sturdy desk clamp or purpose-made stand for a wheel; follow its mounting instructions and check desk stability.

04 / RETEST

Practice a firm stop safely

Before driving, press the brake firmly several times while stationary. If the chair, desk, or pedals shift, stop and secure them before continuing. Take breaks and change posture if discomfort builds.

Simple test: you should be able to press the brake firmly while your back stays supported, your hips stay in place, and the chair and pedals do not move.
