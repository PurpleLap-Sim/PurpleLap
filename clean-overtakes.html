05 / RACECRAFT

Plan a clean overtake.

A good pass is prepared, visible, and finishable. It is not just a late dive toward the inside: you need a realistic braking plan, enough overlap to share the corner, and a safe exit. If one of those pieces is missing, waiting for another opportunity is part of good racecraft.

A pass starts before the straight.

Look for a repeatable advantage rather than a single desperate braking move.

Earn a closing speed

Stay close enough to benefit from a better exit, but leave room to react if the car ahead brakes early. Focus on a smooth exit from the previous turn; a small speed advantage carried down the straight is easier to control than a last-second lunge.

Choose the side and commit

Check mirrors, relative-position indicators, and the other driver’s movement. Move toward the passing side early and make one clear move. Avoid weaving or changing sides in the braking zone; leave a safe route if the gap closes.

Share the corner, then sort it out.

Once cars are alongside, both drivers have less room. Stay predictable and make space.

Be alongside before the turn

Do not assume the other driver has seen you. Establish meaningful overlap before the corner and keep your car under control. If you arrive too late to make the turn without contact or cutting the track, lift and try again later.

Keep a car-width of space

When a car is alongside, leave it racing room through the turn and on exit. Hold a steady line rather than squeezing it toward the edge. The exact sporting rules differ among games and leagues, so learn the local rules before racing.

Finish before you move back

After the corner, check that you are fully clear before crossing back toward the racing line. If you are still side by side, continue sharing the track. Avoid braking suddenly or cutting across the other car’s nose.

Know when to abandon it

Rain, cold tires, dirty air, damage, a closing gap, or an unexpected defense can make the pass unsafe. Ease off, leave a buffer, and reset for the next straight. A clean second attempt is better than contact or a penalty.

Choose a pass you can finish.

Different corners reward different approaches. Commit only when the speed, space, and exit plan all make sense.

Think about the exit first

An inside move may reach the apex sooner but leave a tighter exit; an outside line can preserve momentum if there is room to stay alongside. Neither is automatically better. Consider the next straight, the other car’s position, and whether both cars can make the turn without contact.

Use a clear braking reference

Following closely can make it harder to judge the braking point, and the car ahead may brake earlier than you expect. Pick your own reference and leave enough margin to stop safely. If you cannot make the corner from the passing line, lift and abandon the attempt.

Know the local overlap rules

Games, leagues, and real-world rule sets can define overlap and racing room differently. Learn the rules for the session you are joining; a position indicator or spotter can help with awareness but does not replace checking that the move is safe.

Make the next move later

After a pass, settle the car and check mirrors or relative-position tools before defending. Do not cut across a rival who is still alongside. One clean position gained is better than immediately risking contact to gain another.

Practice without pressure.

Use a private session or easy AI on a familiar circuit. Try following closely for a lap, then attempt a pass only when you can arrive alongside before turn-in and still make the corner. Review one attempt afterward: Was the move visible? Was there overlap? Did both cars have room at the exit?
