09 / RACE PLANNING

Make a plan. Adapt it.

Fuel and pit strategy are about balancing time lost in the pit lane against the benefit of fresh tires, repairs, or a lighter car. The right choice depends on the simulator’s rules, race length, fuel model, tire behavior, and traffic. Build a plan from your own practice data, then update it when the race changes.

Build the plan from evidence.

Use practice or a private session to estimate real consumption and pit time for the event you are entering.

FUEL

Measure consumption safely

Run several representative laps and note fuel used per lap. Include the start, traffic, and any extra formation or cooldown laps required by the session. Add a sensible reserve instead of planning to finish at exactly zero.

PIT LOSS

Know what a stop costs

Check the pit-lane time, speed limit, service time, and whether refueling or tire changes happen together or separately. A short race may not justify a stop; a longer one may make a fresh set or repair worth the delay.

WINDOW

Choose a flexible stop range

Estimate when fuel, tire condition, or race rules make a stop necessary, but keep a window rather than a single lap. Traffic, a caution period, weather changes, or damage can shift the best time.

EXECUTION

Prepare the pit entry

Know where the speed limit begins, select the service you need, and avoid changing settings while approaching the pit lane. Follow the simulator’s pit procedure and do not cross a line or ignore a penalty to save time.

ILLUSTRATIVE STINT PLAN · ESTIMATE FROM YOUR SIM AND EVENT RULES

Rules come before strategy.

Some events mandate stops, restrict refueling, or specify tire compounds and service. Check the session rules first. Do not assume real-world procedures or strategy advice applies to every game mode.

Practice: test one race-length plan

In a private session, measure fuel use over several clean laps and confirm the pit-lane time with the same car and event rules. Estimate a reserve, then compare a no-stop run with a planned stop if both are legal. Record the assumptions so you can revise the plan when pace, traffic, or conditions change.
