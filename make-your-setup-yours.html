03 / YOUR EQUIPMENT

Make your setup yours.

A good setup is one that lets you drive comfortably and consistently. You can learn a lot with a controller, and a wheel is an option when you’re ready. Start with what you own, then change one thing at a time.

NO EXPENSIVE RIG REQUIRED

Choose your starting point.

There’s no required shopping list. Each option can work; the best one is the one that fits your space, budget, and what you enjoy.

01 / ALREADY OWN ONE?

Controller

A controller is enough to learn braking, steering, racing lines, and racecraft. Use small, smooth inputs and adjust sensitivity if the game feels twitchy.

02 / WHEN YOU’RE READY

Wheel

A wheel can make steering feel more direct and immersive. Check that the exact model works with your console or computer and the game you play before buying anything.

03 / KEEP IT STEADY

Seat, desk & pedals

A stable seat and a comfortable reach matter more than a fancy cockpit. Keep pedals from sliding, and set your screen where you can see it without leaning forward.

Small changes. Better sessions.

Make the setup feel predictable before chasing a perfect setting. Keep notes so you can undo a change that does not help.

01

Get comfortable first

Sit so your shoulders stay relaxed and you can reach the wheel or controller without stretching.

02

Start with gentle settings

Use a manageable steering sensitivity and force feedback level, then make one small adjustment at a time.

03

Calibrate before you tune

Follow the game’s controller or wheel calibration steps. Check that steering and pedals reach their full range.

04

Save what works

Keep a note or screenshot of settings that feel good. Change one value at a time so you know what made a difference.

YOUR FIRST TEN MINUTES

A simple setup check.

Pick a familiar car and track, then practise at a comfortable pace. Aim for settings and posture you can repeat, not the fastest lap straight away.

  • Place the screen where it’s easy to see and keep your seat stable.
  • Check that your steering, throttle, and brake respond smoothly.
  • Choose a comfortable force feedback level if you use a wheel.
  • Do a few laps, then adjust just one thing if needed.

READY TO MOVE ON?

Read the racing line.

Learn how braking points, turn-in, apexes, and exits work together to make a smoother corner.
