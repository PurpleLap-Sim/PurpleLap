04 / CORNER CRAFT

Read the racing line.

The racing line is a useful path through a corner, not a painted rule you must follow. It links a controllable entry to an apex and an exit that gives you space and speed for what comes next. Learn the reference points first; adjust the line to the corner, car, and conditions.

BRAKE · RELEASE · ROTATE · UNWIND

Four references, one smooth path.

Pick a fixed visual marker for each phase. Repeatability makes it easier to learn what a change actually did.

01 / BRAKING POINT

Slow down while the car is settled

Choose a board, shadow, barrier seam, or other visible reference. Brake in a straight line where possible, using a marker you can find every lap. Start earlier than you think, then move the point gradually only when you can make the corner consistently.

02 / TURN-IN

Turn when the speed is ready

Release the brake smoothly and begin steering toward the corner. Turning too early can make you run out of road at the exit; turning too late can force a sharp, slow angle. Change either the braking point or turn-in point at a time, not both together.

03 / APEX

Clip the useful part of the inside

The apex is the point where your path passes nearest the inside of the corner. A late apex can open the exit and help you accelerate sooner. An early apex can leave you running wide. You do not need to touch the curb if it unsettles the car or breaks track limits.

04 / EXIT

Unwind steering as you add power

Let the car use the available exit width while staying within the marked track. More steering angle uses tire grip that could otherwise accelerate the car, so progressively unwind the wheel as the car straightens. Make sure your exit position sets up the next turn.

Think beyond a single corner.

If a corner leads onto a long straight, prioritize a clean exit even if that means a later apex and a slower entry. In a sequence, position the car for the next turn rather than maximizing the first one in isolation. A defensive or passing line may also differ from the fastest solo line.

Practice: make one corner repeatable

Choose one corner and keep the car, assists, and setup unchanged. For five laps, focus on the same braking reference. Then adjust just one thing—such as braking a little earlier or using a later apex—and compare whether the car exits straighter and reaches power sooner. If you miss the marker, reset and try again rather than forcing the corner.
