02 / THE FOUNDATION

Find your first rhythm.

The fastest lap isn’t always made by driving with big aggression. Clean, consistent racing is the key: make the car predictable, then build speed one small step at a time.

OUTSIDE · APEX · OUTSIDEKEEP IT SMOOTH

Three things to focus on.

Pick one track and one car. Keep the same settings while you practise so it’s easier to notice what you’re improving.

01 / LOOK AHEAD

Look where you want to go

Try to look toward the next part of the track instead of staring right in front of the car. This gives you more time to react.

02 / BRAKE IN A STRAIGHT LINE

Slow down before the turn

Choose a clear marker, like a board or a change in the barrier. Brake before the corner, then ease off as you turn in.

03 / BE SMOOTH

Make one clean turn

A useful starting point is to begin wide, move toward the inside near the middle, then let the car run back out. The best line can change with the corners around it.

READ THE WHOLE SECTION

Corners work together.

The same turn can need a different line depending on what comes just before and after it. A previous corner may affect the side of the track you arrive from. If this turn leads onto a long straight, a later apex can help you get on the power sooner. If another turn follows quickly, you may need to give up a little space here to set up the next one. Look at the sequence, not just one corner at a time.

WHEN A LAP GOES WRONG

Change one thing at a time.

If you miss the apex, do not immediately brake later and turn harder on the next lap. First decide what happened: did you arrive too fast, brake too late, or turn in too early? Pick one small adjustment and keep the rest of the lap the same. If you run wide on exit, lift smoothly, stay within the track limits, and use the next lap to test a slightly earlier slowdown or later apex.

A BETTER FIRST TARGET

Repeat five calm laps.

Ignore lap time for the first set. Note whether you stayed on track, used the same braking reference, and made fewer corrections. Once most laps look alike, change one corner entry or exit and compare the result. A lap timer becomes useful when you can explain what changed—not when it only tells you that one lap was quicker.

A simple first practice

Drive five laps at a comfortable pace. For the first few, focus on staying on track. Then choose one corner and try to brake at the same marker each lap. If you make a mistake, reset and try again—every lap is useful practice.
